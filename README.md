# Project Anchorage — Microsoft's Acquisition of Activision Blizzard, Inc.

### An Independent M&A Deal Analysis

**By Prerna Dudhmande**

---

## Overview

This repository contains a full-scope, independently researched M&A deal analysis of Microsoft Corporation's $68.7 billion acquisition of Activision Blizzard, Inc. — the largest transaction in video game industry history and the subject of the most extensive multi-jurisdictional antitrust review yet seen in technology M&A.

The project is built end-to-end the way a deal team would package it: a full deal memorandum, a client-style pitchbook, and a complete valuation and merger-modeling suite in Excel — all sourced from SEC filings, court opinions, and company disclosures rather than secondary summaries.

**Note:** This is an independent, illustrative analysis prepared for educational and portfolio purposes. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or produced on behalf of Microsoft Corporation, Activision Blizzard, Inc., or any financial institution referenced in the materials. It does not constitute investment advice.

### 01 — Deal Analysis Report
An 18-section, ~50-page report covering the acquirer and target, industry overview, transaction structure, strategic rationale, synergy analysis, valuation, financial impact, the three-jurisdiction regulatory contest (FTC / CMA / European Commission), integration challenges, risk assessment, and post-acquisition performance through 2025.

### 02 — Pitchbook
A 20-slide presentation covering the same analysis in client-ready form: native charts, a valuation football field, a regulatory process flowchart, a risk matrix, and a SWOT grid.

### 03 — Financial Models
Four fully formula-linked Excel workbooks:
- **DCF Model** — 5-year unlevered FCF build, WACC (CAPM) derivation, terminal value, and a WACC/growth sensitivity grid
- **Comparable Companies Analysis** — EA, Take-Two, Ubisoft, and Activision Blizzard trading multiples
- **Comparable Transactions Analysis** — six precedent gaming M&A deals with implied multiples
- **Financial Model (Merger Analysis)** — sources & uses, purchase price allocation (reconciled to Microsoft's actual disclosed FY2024 PPA), pro forma income statement combination, and EPS accretion/dilution

Every workbook follows standard modeling convention (blue = input, black = formula, formulas fully linked, checks tie to zero) and has been recalculated and verified to contain zero formula errors.

### 04 — Executive Summary
A standalone 2-page version of the analysis for quick reading.

### 05 — Career Materials
Resume bullets, a LinkedIn post, and interview talking points written to accompany this project during recruiting conversations.

## Key Analytical Findings

- **The announced price wasn't the final price.** Microsoft's actual closing consideration was $75.4bn — $6.7bn above the $68.7bn figure announced in January 2022 — due to share dilution over the 21-month regulatory review.
- **Purchase accounting distorted near-term results.** Microsoft's own 10-K shows Activision Blizzard contributed an *operating loss* in the first partial year post-close, driven by intangible amortization and a deferred-revenue haircut — not underlying business deterioration.
- **Three regulators, three outcomes on the same facts.** The U.S. FTC lost in federal court; the UK CMA issued an outright prohibition later reversed via a structural divestiture; the European Commission cleared the deal via a behavioral remedy.
- **The DCF and precedent-transaction analysis both suggest the $95.00 offer was full but not excessive** — within the trading comparables range and below the average multiple paid in comparable recent gaming M&A.
